## 1. What to look for

| # | Topic and questions to answer | Research question | Covers |
|---|-------------------------------|-------------------|--------|
| T1 | **The season start:** how and when do Berlin cafés announce that seasonal drinks are back, and in which places (board, website, social media)? | RQ1 | U3, A7 |
| T2 | **Finding channels:** where do people in Berlin look for cafés and seasonal drinks, and what does each channel show or leave out (availability, price, ingredients)? | RQ1, RQ5 | U4, A7 |
| T3 | **What is in the drink:** what are pumpkin spice sauces and syrups usually made of? Real pumpkin or flavouring, dairy in the sauce, made in-house or bought in? | RQ2, RQ7 | U5, U6, A3, A8 |
| T4 | **Allergen disclosure rules:** what must cafés in Germany disclose about allergens in drinks that are not pre-packed, and in what form (written, on request, on the menu)? | RQ2, RQ7 | U6, A8 |
| T5 | **Chains vs independents:** what does each publish about seasonal drinks (ingredient lists, allergens, price, availability), and through which channels? | RQ6, RQ7 | U7, A4, A5 |
| T6 | **Doing without the café:** how are home-made versions and ready-made syrups for home use presented: recipes, ingredients, effort, cost? | RQ5 | U4 |
| T7 | **Analogous domains:** how do other products with changing menus or hidden ingredients communicate availability and contents (seasonal produce, rotating menus, allergen menus)? | RQ1, RQ2 | A7, A8 |
| T8 | **Supplier information:** what do syrup and sauce suppliers make available to cafés (spec sheets, allergen declarations, ingredient lists)? | RQ7 | U6, A8, U10 |

T8 looks at what information exists for cafés to pass on. It does not cover café-side tools or dashboards (a scope exclusion in `00_Brief.md`).

## 2. Where to look

| Source type | Why it fits this domain | Topics |
|-------------|-------------------------|--------|
| Café and chain menus, websites and apps (drink and ingredient pages) | They show what buyers can see before ordering, in the café's own words | T1, T3, T5 |
| Café social media accounts and Berlin café guides and listings | This is where independent cafés announce seasonal drinks and where Berlin buyers search | T1, T2 |
| Supplier product pages, spec sheets and allergen declarations for syrups and sauces | They are the first-hand record of what bought-in sauces contain | T3, T8 |
| German and EU food-information regulation and consumer-advice guidance | They set what cafés must disclose about allergens in loose drinks | T4 |
| Recipe blogs, food media and home-cooking forums | They show the home-made alternative and how people compare it with the café version | T6 |
| Hospitality and coffee trade press | It covers how cafés source, price and promote seasonal drinks, and how other menus handle changing offers | T5, T7, T8 |

## 3. How to assess

1. **Germany, Berlin and the last two seasons:** allergen rules, menus and prices differ by country and change every season. Treat anything outside Germany, or older than the two seasons the brief covers, as context only.
2. **First-hand record over marketing copy:** ingredient claims from a spec sheet, ingredient list or legal declaration count more than promotional wording. What is really in the drink is the question being checked (A3, A8).
3. **Chain or independent:** note which kind of café each source describes. Kiezkarte works only with independent cafés (D08), so sources about chains are context, not evidence about partner cafés.

**Not for desk research:** the main question (why people don't buy, and which reason matters most), RQ3 (how buyers judge value), RQ4 (what the drink means to buyers), RQ8 (the moment the purchase falls apart). All four need participants.
