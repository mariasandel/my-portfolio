# Brief Interrogation: Pumpkin Spice Latte in Berlin

Source: `00 Context/00_Brief.md` · Date: 2026-10-13

## Knowns

| # | Known | Evidence quote |
|---|-------|----------------|
| K1 | The client is Kiezkarte, a Berlin start-up that works with independent cafés | "**Client:** Kiezkarte, a Berlin start-up that works with independent cafés" |
| K2 | The product is a mobile-first service for coffee buyers in Berlin | "**Product:** A mobile-first service for coffee buyers in Berlin (iOS and Android)" |
| K3 | The sprint is 2 weeks and the output is a concept and prototype | "**Sprint duration:** 2 weeks" / "**Output:** Concept and prototype (Figma Make or Claude Design)" |

## Unknowns

| # | Unknown | Why it matters |
|---|---------|----------------|
| U1 | Which of the three reasons (finding, contents, price) actually stops people from buying? | This is the core of the synthesis |
| U2 | Are the reasons the same for everyone, or do different buyers stop for different reasons? | Decides whether one intervention can serve everyone, or whether we need personas |
| U3 | At which moment does the purchase fall apart: before leaving home, on the way, or at the counter? | The intervention has to sit at a specific moment |
| U4 | What do buyers do today to work around the problem (Instagram, chain apps, asking staff, making it at home)? | Workarounds show unmet needs and the alternatives we compete with |
| U5 | How much does a dietary need (lactose, vegan) shape the decision? | Could turn a "nice to know" into a blocker |
| U6 | Do independent cafés know what is in their own syrups and sauces? | If staff can't answer, information has to come from somewhere else |
| U7 | How do buyers compare independents and chains? | Chains are the reference buyers know, even though Kiezkarte can't partner with them |
| U8 | What does the drink mean to people: a treat, a habit, a ritual? | Shapes what "worth it" means |
| U9 | How were the four participants recruited? | Affects how far findings can be generalised |
| U10 | Is there any data beyond the interviews (café sales, walk-outs, search data)? | Without it, claims of importance stay qualitative |

## Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Risk | Could research challenge it? |
|---|------------|------|------------------------------|
| A1 | That people really want the drink and are being stopped, rather than not wanting it much | High | ⚑ Yes: participants may care less than the café reports suggest |
| A2 | That one "most important" reason can be found from four interviews | High | ⚑ Yes: each participant may stop for a different reason |
| A3 | That the problem is information (where, what, how much) and not the product itself | High | ⚑ Yes: the drink may simply not be good enough for the price |
| A4 | That independent cafés are where the problem is worst | Medium | ⚑ Yes: chain buyers may struggle too, or not at all |
| A5 | That buyers trust independent cafés more than chains | Medium | ⚑ Yes: chains may be more transparent (apps, ingredient lists) |
| A6 | That price matters to everyone | Medium | ⚑ Yes: for some, a once-a-year ritual may override price |
| A7 | That a mobile service is where buyers would look | Medium | ⚑ Yes: they may rely on chalkboards, Instagram or word of mouth |
| A8 | That café staff can supply accurate ingredient data | Medium | ⚑ Yes: staff may not know what is in bought-in syrups |
| A9 | That 3–6 screens can change a decision made at the counter | Low | ⚑ Yes: if the decision is made in seconds, screens may come too late |
| A10 | That the pumpkin spice latte stands for seasonal drinks in general | Low | Partially: it may be a special case because of its US/chain image |
