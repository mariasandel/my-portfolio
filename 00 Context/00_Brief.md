# The brief

**Client:** Kiezkarte, a Berlin start-up that works with independent cafés
**Product:** A mobile-first service for coffee buyers in Berlin (iOS and Android)
**Sprint duration:** 2 weeks
**Design challenge:** Buying a seasonal drink, the pumpkin spice latte, at Berlin cafés
**Output:** Concept and prototype (Figma Make or Claude Design)

## The Challenge

Every autumn, the pumpkin spice latte comes back to Berlin. Chains advertise it loudly. Many independent cafés sell their own version, or refuse to sell it at all. Kiezkarte's partner cafés report the same pattern: people come in asking for it, look at the board, ask a question or two, and often leave without ordering.

Kiezkarte wants to understand why. Is it that people can't find it, don't trust what is in it, or don't think it is worth the price? And which of these matters most?

You are not being asked to design a café app or a loyalty programme. You are being asked to find the most important reason a wanted purchase doesn't happen, and design a credible response to it.

## What You Are Designing

You will design one focused intervention: a small set of connected screens or touchpoints that addresses the core problem you identify in your research synthesis.

The theme is defined: buying a seasonal drink in Berlin. Within that theme, which specific problem you focus on is your synthesis decision.

Possible focus areas (not prescriptive — your research should determine which):

- Finding it (where and when it is sold, which cafés have it this season)
- Knowing what is in it (ingredients, real pumpkin or syrup, allergens and dairy)
- Deciding whether it is worth it (price, taste, what you get for €6.50)
- The start of the season (knowing it is back)

## Constraints

- **Platform:** Mobile-first. iOS primary, with Android parity considered.
- **Partners:** Independent cafés. Chains are part of the market buyers know, but Kiezkarte does not work with them.
- **Tools:** Claude Code, Claude Design, Figma Make.
- **Fidelity:** Low to mid fidelity. Clarity over polish.
- **Scope:** 3–6 connected screens. Not a full café-finder or ordering app.
- **Scope exclusions:** Café-side tools and dashboards, payments and ordering, loyalty and rewards, delivery.

## Research Input

The research dataset is in `01 Discover/Primary Research/Raw/`:

- 4 semi-structured interviews with people in Berlin who have bought, or tried to buy, a pumpkin spice latte in the last two seasons
- Participants: Lena (34), Tobias (23), Aiyana (29), Jonas (31)
- Theme: finding, trusting and paying for a seasonal drink

## What Success Looks Like

At the end of the sprint, you should have:

- A synthesized problem statement (need statement + selected HMW with success statements) supported by evidence from the research
- 2 proto-personas grounded in the research
- A set of distinct concepts, and a prototype of the chosen one
- A short design rationale: what you designed, why, and which research finding it addresses

The prototype does not need to be complete. It needs to be clear enough that someone could interact with it and understand the design direction.

## Assessment Criteria ⚠️

- **Research grounding:** is every design decision traceable to a specific research finding?
- **Problem framing quality:** is the problem statement specific to this research, or could it apply to any product?
- **Prototype clarity:** can someone interact with your screens and understand your design direction without a verbal walkthrough?
- **AI judgment:** where did you evaluate and override an AI output, and can you name the criterion you used?
- **Scope discipline:** does your design stay within 3–6 connected screens that address one focused problem?
- **Rationale:** does your design rationale explain what you designed, why, and which research finding it directly addresses?
